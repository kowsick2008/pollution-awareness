Major Health & Environmental Harms

  • Respiratory Illnesses: Breathing in particulate matter and toxic gases damages the lungs, causing diseases like asthma, chronic bronchitis, and emphysema.
  • Cardiovascular Diseases: Fine pollutants (like PM2.5) can enter the bloodstream, triggering inflammation, heart attacks, strokes, and premature death.
  • Neurological Damage: Long-term exposure to heavy air toxins like lead and carbon monoxide can damage brain cells, impairing cognitive function and nerve health.
  • Environmental Acidification: Air pollutants combine with atmospheric moisture to form acid rain, which destroys forests, harms aquatic life, and erodes buildings.
  • Global Climate Change: Greenhouse gases trapped in the atmosphere drive global warming, resulting in extreme weather events and rising sea levels.
